# 10. Business Model

## Model philosophy

MamaSafe AI is a commercial product with a mission constraint, not a charity with a website. Sustainable revenue funds the clinical-content governance, safety evaluation, and localization work that a free hobby project cannot maintain. At the same time, one principle is non-negotiable: **essential emergency functionality stays affordable for the facilities that need it most.** A primary-health center's ability to run the PPH pathway during an emergency must never depend on its procurement budget that month.

## Customer segments

| Segment | What they buy | Why it is worth paying for |
|---|---|---|
| **Private hospitals and clinic networks** | Facility subscription: full workflow suite, audit/quality dashboards, staff simulation training | Quality improvement, documentation completeness, staff training value, medico-legal defensibility of structured records |
| **State ministries of health / federal programs** | Government licensing: deployment across PHC networks with configuration to local protocols, analytics at population level (de-identified, aggregated) | System-wide emergency-care consistency; referral quality; training leverage across thousands of workers; alignment with maternal-mortality reduction mandates |
| **NGOs and maternal-health organizations** | NGO-supported deployment: sponsored licenses for facilities they support, plus program reporting | Measurable program outcomes (simulation metrics first, pilot metrics later); offline capability fits field operations |
| **Nursing schools, medical schools, midwifery and anesthesia training programs** | Training licenses: simulation mode, scenario libraries, evaluation dashboards for learners | Scalable emergency training without mannequin-lab capital costs; repeatable, multilingual practice |
| **Development organizations / funders** | Multi-country deployment programs | A fundable, measurable knowledge-delivery intervention aligned with SDG 3.1 and the WHO PPH Roadmap (2024) |
| **Platform partners (later)** | API licensing of the rule engine, knowledge system, or audit layer | Reuse of governed clinical infrastructure in adjacent products |

## Revenue streams

1. **Facility subscriptions** — tiered by facility size and module set. The entry tier (PPH + escalation + referral documentation, offline) is priced for small facilities; higher tiers add modules (preeclampsia/eclampsia, sepsis, newborn), dashboards, and admin features.
2. **Government licensing** — negotiated state/federal agreements with protocol configuration, training rollout, and support; the natural scale engine in Nigeria's public system.
3. **NGO deployment partnerships** — sponsored deployments with outcome reporting; NGOs fund, facilities receive, we deliver and measure.
4. **Training licenses** — simulation-mode subscriptions for education institutions; a recurring-revenue stream that also builds the next generation's familiarity with the product.
5. **Freemium clinician education** — free educational content and limited simulation to build trust and distribution; emergency workflows for registered facilities.
6. **API licensing (later)** — governed access to platform components once the evaluation and audit infrastructure is battle-tested.

## The affordability commitment, made concrete

- Emergency-recognition and core PPH workflow functionality will always be available at a price a rural PHC can bear — subsidized where necessary by NGO/government programs and cross-subsidized by premium tiers.
- Simulation-mode training content for students is priced for institutions, not individual students.
- No per-emergency or per-use pricing. We will never meter a hemorrhage.

## Unit economics logic (qualitative at this stage)

- **Low marginal delivery cost:** offline-first PWA/Android distribution avoids heavy per-facility infrastructure; cloud costs arise mainly from optional Gemini features, which are metered into higher tiers.
- **High retention drivers:** guideline-version governance, audit records, and training dashboards create ongoing value that pure content apps lack.
- **Land-and-expand:** PPH proves value → additional maternal modules → pediatric and platform modules expand contract size within the same facility relationship.
- **Government/NGO contracts** provide multi-year revenue visibility that funds the safety and evaluation pipeline.

## Go-to-market sequence

1. **Phase 0 (now):** working demo + simulation evidence; conversations with maternal-health NGOs, training institutions, and innovation units of state ministries — using the pilot proposal (asset 14) as the door-opener.
2. **Phase 1 (post-Lab):** simulation-based deployments in 2–3 training institutions and 5–10 partner facilities for usability and workflow validation (no patient use).
3. **Phase 2:** ethically governed pilot deployments (shadow mode, then supported use) with a state ministry or NGO partner; first government license negotiated on pilot evidence.
4. **Phase 3:** multi-state expansion, additional modules, and second-country entry with a development partner.

## Pricing posture vs. alternatives

MamaSafe AI competes against "free" (PDFs, posters, informal chatbot use) and against scarce capital-intensive options (simulation centers, EMR suites). The pitch to buyers is not "pay for an app"; it is "pay for governed, cited, auditable emergency-capability infrastructure that works offline and trains your staff continuously — for a fraction of one mannequin lab."

## What we deliberately avoid

- No advertising, no data monetization, no sale of clinical data — ever.
- No claims-based marketing (we sell measured workflow capability, not promised mortality reductions).
- No pricing that gates emergency functionality behind a facility's ability to pay in the moment of care.
