# 4. Founder Story

> Note for reviewers: this narrative is written from the founder's documented background (CRNA/anesthesia clinician, healthcare researcher, AI builder, founder of the safety-first AnesthesiaOS platform, with a stated commitment to solving healthcare problems in Nigeria and across Africa). It contains no invented biographical incidents.

## Where the story starts: the head of the bed

I am a Certified Registered Nurse Anesthetist — a clinician whose professional home is the most time-compressed, information-dense corner of medicine. In anesthesia, emergencies do not announce themselves politely. A hemorrhage becomes a hemodynamic crisis in minutes. An airway becomes a catastrophe in seconds. Survival depends on three things: recognizing deterioration early, executing a well-rehearsed sequence under pressure, and communicating precisely with the team around you.

Over years of high-acuity anesthesia and perioperative practice, I internalized a lesson that has become the architectural spine of everything I build: **in a crisis, nobody rises to the occasion — they fall to the level of their systems.** Checklists, dosing tables, cognitive aids, escalation criteria, read-backs, and closed-loop communication are not bureaucracy. They are how fallible humans deliver reliable care at 3 a.m. when they are tired, alone, or afraid.

## The question that would not leave me

I have also spent years studying healthcare systems in Nigeria and across Africa — as a researcher, and as someone with a deep personal commitment to the continent's health. The numbers stopped being abstract a long time ago: roughly 82,000 Nigerian women died of maternal causes in 2020 — over a quarter of the world's total [UN MMEIG country profile for Nigeria, 2023]. Hemorrhage is the leading cause of maternal death globally, about 27% of all maternal deaths [Cresswell et al., WHO systematic analysis, *Lancet Global Health*, 2025].

I kept asking the same question: **what does a postpartum hemorrhage look like in a primary-health center in rural Nigeria at 2 a.m.?** I know exactly what it looks like in a well-resourced operating room: a team, blood products, a massive-transfusion protocol, an anesthetist at the head of the bed. In too many African facilities it looks like one midwife, one shelf of supplies, a patchy phone signal, a protocol on a poster, and a referral hospital hours away. The clinical knowledge to save that woman exists — WHO has published it, the WOMAN trial proved TXA works when given within three hours [WOMAN Trial Collaborators, *Lancet* 2017] — but knowledge in a PDF does not resuscitate anyone.

The constraint is not only the number of clinicians. It is that the clinicians who exist are asked to perform expert-level emergency reasoning under crushing time, information, staffing, referral, and connectivity constraints. I could not train a million new specialists. But I realized I might be able to put the *systems* of the operating room — the checklist, the dosing logic, the escalation criteria, the structured handoff — into the pocket of every frontline worker, in a form that works without the internet and speaks their language.

## Why I am the person to build this

I bring an unusual combination, each piece earned rather than claimed:

- **Clinical credibility in exactly the right emergencies.** Hemorrhage resuscitation, hemodynamic crisis, medication safety, airway emergencies, and perioperative deterioration are my daily professional substance. I know what a shock index of 1.4 feels like at the bedside, and I know why an ergometrine contraindication in a hypertensive patient is not a footnote.
- **Safety-engineering discipline.** Through AnesthesiaOS — my safety-first perioperative AI platform — I have spent years formalizing what responsible clinical AI architecture means: simulation-only development, mandatory provider oversight, recommendations that are never commands, immutable audit trails, version control, and honest regulatory posture. That discipline is inherited whole by MamaSafe AI.
- **Research rigor.** I am a healthcare researcher and scholarly writer. This application contains no unverifiable statistic, no mortality-reduction claim, and no fabricated capability — because that is how I am trained to work, and because clinical AI that exaggerates is dangerous.
- **Builder's execution.** I design and ship software. MamaSafe AI is not a slide deck: the postpartum hemorrhage workflow runs today, in simulation, offline, with a deterministic rule engine underneath and an audit trail behind it.
- **Mission fit.** This is not a market I discovered; it is a commitment I have held for years. Nigeria is the starting point because the need is greatest and the leverage is largest — not because it is convenient.

## The conviction

Every maternal death from hemorrhage in an understaffed facility is, in part, a **systems failure** — a failure to deliver already-known, already-published, already-proven knowledge to the point of care at the moment of need. That is a solvable problem. MamaSafe AI is my answer: verified medical knowledge, converted into offline, actionable clinical workflows, multiplying the capabilities of the health workers Africa already has. The AI assists. The clinician — always — decides.
